All numbers, whether it be binary or hexadecimal, are ultimately represented and
interpreted as binary by a computer.

Bitwise shift operations:

In binary, a bitwise shift tells us precisely the number of times to shift left
or right. This looks like as follows:

000100 >> 1 = 000010 (right shift)
000100 << 1 = 001000 (left shift)

In hexadecimal, a number F000 is, under the hood, binary for a computer. It is
represented as follows:

1111 0000 0000 0000 = F 0 0 0

Because we need exactly 4 bits to represent 0-F in binary. As such, a bitwise
shift of 4 is required to shift once in hexadecimal.

000F00 >> 4 = 0000F0
000F00 << 4 = 00F000

To shift more than once, shift by a multiple of 4. To shift twice, shift by
4 * 2 = 8 times. To shift thrice, shift by 4 * 3 = 12 times.
